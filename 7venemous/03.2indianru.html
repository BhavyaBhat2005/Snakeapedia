Indian Russell's Viper

Overview

  • The Indian Russell's viper (Daboia russelii) is a highly venomous snake found across India and parts of Southeast Asia.
  • It has a distinct chain-like pattern on its body and is known for its loud hissing when threatened.
  • Often found in grasslands and farmlands, it is responsible for many snakebite cases due to accidental encounters.
  • Its venom is hemotoxic, affecting blood clotting and causing severe health complications.
  • Quick medical treatment with antivenom is essential for survival.
  • Apart from its medical significance, the Russell's viper plays an important role in controlling rodent populations, contributing to the balance of local ecosystems.

Physical Characteristics

  • The Indian Russell's viper (Daboia russelii) has a stout, robust body with a distinct triangular head that is clearly broader than the neck.
  • Its body color ranges from yellowish-brown to tan, featuring three rows of dark brown or black oval spots outlined in white or light yellow along its back, giving it a chain-like appearance.
  • The head bears two dark patches, and the snout is blunt.
  • Its eyes have vertical pupils, typical of vipers, and it possesses large, hinged fangs used to inject venom.
  • The scales are keeled, giving the skin a rough texture.
  • Adult snakes typically measure between 4 to 5 feet (1.2 to 1.5 meters) in length, though some individuals can grow longer.

Habitat

  • The Russell's viper (Daboia russelii) thrives in a variety of habitats, making it one of the most widely distributed venomous snakes in India and Southeast Asia.
  • It is commonly found in open grassy areas, farmlands, scrublands, and bushy regions, where it hunts for rodents and other prey.
  • The snake is often spotted near human settlements due to the abundance of rodents, which increases the risk of human encounters.
  • Although it prefers dry regions, it can also adapt to coastal lowlands and forest edges but avoids dense forests and extreme altitudes like the Himalayan regions.

Diet and Behavior

  • The Russell's viper (Daboia russelii) primarily feeds on rodents, helping control their populations in agricultural areas.
  • Its diet also includes lizards, frogs, birds, and small mammals.
  • Mostly nocturnal, it hunts at night but may be active during the day in cooler weather.
  • Known for its aggressive behavior, the snake coils and hisses loudly when threatened, striking swiftly if provoked.
  • Unlike many snakes, it often remains still when approached, leading to accidental encounters with humans.
  • It is also a strong swimmer and can climb low vegetation, though it prefers to stay on the ground.

Venom

  • The Russell's viper (Daboia russelii) possesses highly potent venom that is primarily hemotoxic, affecting blood clotting and causing extensive tissue damage.
  • Envenomation can lead to severe symptoms such as pain, swelling, bleeding, blistering, and a drop in blood pressure.
  • In more serious cases, it can cause kidney failure, internal bleeding, and, if left untreated, death.
  • The venom also contains components that can affect the nervous system, leading to breathing difficulties and paralysis in some cases.
  • Prompt medical attention and administration of the appropriate antivenom are crucial for survival.
  • Interestingly, the venom's ability to affect blood coagulation makes it valuable in medical research for studying blood-related disorders.
